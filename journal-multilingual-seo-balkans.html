Multilingual SEO
for Southeast Europe

Croatian, Bosnian and Serbian audiences understand one another. Search engines still need each market to be structured clearly.

FIELD NOTE / 03

For organisations expanding across Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Slovenia, Montenegro and North Macedonia.

Regional web design guide ↗

Multilingual SEO in the Balkans looks simple until a website has to rank. Croatian, Bosnian, Serbian and Montenegrin are mutually understandable, while Serbian also uses Cyrillic. Businesses often publish one regional page and assume it will serve every market. Users may understand it, but the page rarely reflects how each audience searches.

Begin with markets, not translations

A multilingual website should start with a market map. Where can the business genuinely sell, deliver or support customers? Which services are available in each location? Which language does the sales team use after the enquiry? These commercial answers determine the website structure.

Direct translation comes later. A Croatian phrase with strong search demand may have a different Serbian equivalent, while the same English industry term may be common in both markets. Keyword research should therefore be completed per market by a native or near-native speaker who understands the service.

Use a clear URL structure

For most regional businesses, language folders are the cleanest option: /hr/, /bs/, /sr/ and /en/. They keep authority on one domain, make analytics easier to compare and give every language version a stable address.

Each important page should have its own title, meta description, headings and body copy. Automatically replacing navigation labels while leaving the main content unchanged does not create a useful language version. Search engines—and visitors—need a complete page.

Tell search engines which version belongs where

Hreflang annotations connect equivalent pages and indicate their intended language or region. A Croatian service page can point to Bosnian, Serbian and English equivalents, while each of those pages points back. The references must be reciprocal and the URLs must be indexable.

Do not automatically redirect every visitor based only on IP address. A Croatian speaker in Austria, a diaspora customer in Germany or a traveller using a foreign network may want a different version. Suggest a language, remember the choice and keep the selector visible.

Good regional SEO recognises linguistic proximity without pretending every market has identical intent.

Localise evidence, not only sentences

A visitor in Sarajevo will trust relevant delivery details, local project examples and prices or processes they can understand. A buyer in Zagreb may expect different terminology or legal information. Local phone formats, currencies, testimonials and service coverage can be stronger ranking and conversion signals than repeating a city name throughout the copy.

This is also where many generated translations fail. Grammatically acceptable text can still sound distant, overly formal or imported. Editorial review matters because trust is carried by small choices: how directly a headline speaks, whether a call to action feels natural and whether a phrase belongs to that market.

Build a Balkan content cluster

A service page alone cannot answer every search. Support it with genuinely useful regional articles: pricing factors for a website in Croatia, choosing a development partner in Bosnia and Herzegovina, multilingual ecommerce considerations for Serbia, or a comparison of local and international project workflows.

Connect those articles with descriptive internal links. The result is a topic cluster that helps search engines understand expertise while giving prospective clients a logical path from research to enquiry. Our guide to web design in the Balkans is an example of a regional pillar article.

Measure each market separately

Track impressions, clicks, landing pages and conversions by language folder and country. A page may attract traffic from one market but generate qualified enquiries in another. That difference should inform the next content investment.

Regional SEO is not a one-time translation project. It is a system of technical signals, local editorial judgment and continued learning. When those parts work together, one website can feel genuinely local across several Southeast European markets without fragmenting the brand.
